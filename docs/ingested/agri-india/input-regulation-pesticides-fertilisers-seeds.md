# Agricultural input regulation: orientation note

Document type: Paraphrased orientation note prepared for this project. NOT legal advice.
Jurisdiction: India
Official source to verify: Insecticides Act, 1968 and Rules, 1971; Fertiliser (Control) Order, 1985; Seeds Act, 1966 and any replacement law; state agriculture department notifications; Central Insecticides Board and Registration Committee.
Status: NEEDS-VALIDATION with legal and regulatory experts. Registrations and bans change.

## Main ideas
- Pesticides: manufacture, sale, and use are regulated; products must be registered and labelled, and approved uses (crop, pest, dose, waiting period) are fixed by registration and label. Some products are banned or restricted.
- Fertilisers: quality standards and sale are controlled by orders at national and state level.
- Seeds: notified varieties and quality (such as certification and labelling) are regulated.
- Dealers and sellers: licensing and record-keeping rules apply to input dealers.
- State differences: state agriculture departments add notifications and local restrictions.

## Why it matters for software
- An advisory or marketplace app that suggests or sells inputs could encourage unregistered, banned, or wrongly used products.
- Product catalogues need verified registration data and label information, kept up to date.
- Online sale of regulated inputs raises licensing and verification questions. NEEDS-VALIDATION.

## Requirement prompts
- What master data source defines allowed products, and how often is it refreshed?
- How does the system block recommendations or sales of banned or unregistered inputs?
- How are label instructions, waiting periods, and safety precautions shown in the farmer's language?
- Who reviews product listings from sellers and how are complaints handled?
- How are batch, expiry, and complaint records kept for traceability and recall?
