# Farmer and field-user context for requirements

Document type: Project-authored context note (not an official source). Use as assumptions to confirm with real user research.

## Typical user constraints
- Connectivity: patchy coverage in fields and villages; data packs may be small; sync should be resumable and tolerant of drops.
- Devices: low-cost Android phones with limited storage and memory, older OS versions, cracked screens, shared among family members; some users have only feature phones.
- Language and literacy: many languages and dialects; users may prefer voice, icons, and short messages over long text; numerals and units vary by region.
- Time: farm work is time-critical; tasks must be short, and notifications must come at useful times.
- Trust: farmers will ignore tools that previously gave wrong advice or delayed payments; clear sources, human contacts, and honest uncertainty build trust.
- Gender and access: women farmers may have less control over devices and bank accounts; design for assisted and shared use without leaking private data.
- Assisted use: FPO agents, input dealers, and kiosk operators often use the app on a farmer's behalf, which raises consent and audit needs.

## Field staff context
Extension officers, surveyors, and loss assessors work outdoors, often offline, with heat, glare, and rain. They need large touch targets, fast photo capture, and reliable sync.

## Seasonality
Demand is spiky around sowing, input purchase, harvest, and calamities. Systems should plan for peaks and for fixed deadlines such as enrolment cut-offs.

## Hardware context
Sensors may fail from moisture, dust, insects, theft, or flat batteries. Calibration drift is common. Gateways may lose power. Requirements should state accuracy, calibration, maintenance, and alert handling.
