# Electronic National Agriculture Market (e-NAM): Official Operational Framework and Assaying Standards

**Document Title:** National Agriculture Market (e-NAM) Operational Guidelines & Quality Assaying Framework  
**Authority:** Small Farmers' Agribusiness Consortium (SFAC) & Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, Ministry of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, Government of India  
**Official Digital Channel:** enam.gov.in  
**Jurisdiction:** Republic of India  
**Document Nature:** Official Market Guidelines, Electronic Trading Rules, and Quality Grading Standards  

---

## 1. Objectives of e-NAM

The National Agriculture Market (e-NAM) is a pan-India electronic trading portal that networks existing Agricultural Produce Market Committee (APMC) mandis to create a unified national market for agricultural commodities. Its primary objectives include:
1. Providing transparent, competitive, real-time price discovery through electronic auctions;
2. Promoting quality-based pricing through scientific, standardized assaying;
3. Enabling direct online payments into farmers' bank accounts, cutting out unauthorized market deductions;
4. Facilitating trade across mandi and state boundaries (inter-mandi and inter-state trade).

---

## 2. Standard Mandi Trading Workflow on e-NAM

Every lot of agricultural produce traded through an e-NAM integrated mandi follows a standardized, six-stage electronic pipeline:

```
1. Gate Entry  -->  2. Quality Assaying  -->  3. Electronic Auction
                                                     |
6. Gate Exit   <--  5. e-Payment Settlement <-- 4. Final Weighment
```

### Stage 1: Gate Entry and Lot Registration
* When a farmer, FPO, or aggregator brings produce to the mandi gate, entry data is recorded electronically:
  * Farmer Name, Farmer ID, contact number, and Aadhaar/Bank details;
  * Commodity, estimated quantity, vehicle registration number, and source village;
* **Lot Generation:** System generates a unique, persistent **Lot ID** and prints a physical barcode/QR code token affixed to the produce batch.

### Stage 2: Scientific Quality Assaying and Grading
* Mandatory sampling is conducted by designated mandi assayers or third-party assaying labs prior to auction.
* Produce is tested against official **AGMARK / Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)** specifications:
  * **Moisture content (%):** Determined using digital grain moisture meters;
  * **Foreign matter / Refraction (%):** Organic and inorganic foreign matter;
  * **Damaged / Discolored / Immature grains (%);**
  * **Weeviled / Insect-damaged grains (%);**
  * **Admixture of other varieties (%).**
* **Digital Assaying Certificate:** Test results are uploaded to e-NAM and linked to the Lot ID. Bidders can view certified quality metrics on their screens before submitting bids.

### Stage 3: Electronic Auction and Price Discovery
* **Single-Window e-Bidding:** Registered traders and buyers view active lots, certified quality assay reports, and farmer reserve prices.
* **Auction Mechanics:** Dynamic, transparent electronic bidding during a designated trading window.
* **Bid Acceptance:** System ranks bids and declares the highest valid bid (H1). The farmer retains the sovereign right to accept or decline the final bid price without penalty.

### Stage 4: Secondary Weighment & Electronic Invoicing
* Upon farmer acceptance, the lot undergoes precise electronic weighment on certified digital weighbridges or electronic scales.
* The system calculates gross weight, tare weight, net weight, and applies the agreed per-quintal auction price.
* An electronic **Sale Agreement (Agreement to Sell)** and standardized **Bill of Supply** are generated automatically, reflecting official mandi market cess and statutory user charges.

### Stage 5: Electronic Payment and Financial Settlement
* **e-Payment Gateway Integration:** Buyers must deposit the full payment amount through integrated banking channels (RTGS, NEFT, Net Banking, or UPI) into the designated mandi clearing/escrow account.
* **Direct Farmer Credit:** The clearing house automatically disburses net sale proceeds directly into the farmer's validated bank account on the same day (T+0 or T+1).
* **Prohibition on Cash:** Cash settlements or arbitrary deductions (e.g., unauthorized commission, excessive refraction cuts) are strictly prohibited on the platform.

### Stage 6: Gate Exit Pass
* Upon confirmed electronic settlement, the e-NAM gateway generates an **Electronic Gate Exit Pass (e-Pass)** containing cryptographic verification codes, authorizing produce removal from the market yard.

## 3. Warehouse-Based Trading & WDRA Integration

* **e-NWR (Electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipts):**
  * Warehouses accredited by the **Warehousing Development and Regulatory Authority (WDRA)** are declared as deemed markets under state APMC acts.
  * Farmers depositing harvested produce in accredited warehouses receive an electronic Negotiable Warehouse Receipt (e-NWR) registered in national repositories (NeRL / CCRL).
* **Pledge Financing & Trade:**
  * Farmers can pledge the e-NWR to secure immediate bank working capital (up to 70% of commodity value) to avoid distress sales during harvest gluts.
  * Produce can be traded directly on e-NAM from the warehouse floor without physically transporting bulky commodities to a physical mandi yard.

---

## 4. FPO Trading Module and Logistics Aggregation

* **FPO Direct Sale:** Farmer Producer Organisations can aggregate produce from smallholder member plots, upload bulk lots with aggregated assay certificates, and trade directly with corporate buyers, exporters, and processors across India.
* **Logistics Aggregator Interface:** Integrates third-party transport and cold-chain providers, enabling electronic booking of freight, GPS tracking of transit consignments, and dynamic logistics quotation.
