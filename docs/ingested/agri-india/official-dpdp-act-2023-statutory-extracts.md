# Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act, 2023): Statutory Extracts for Digital Systems

**Document Title:** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (Act No. 22 of 2023)  
**Authority:** Ministry of Law and Justice (Legislative Department) / Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology (MeitY), Government of India  
**Gazette Reference:** The Gazette of India, Extraordinary, Part II—Section 1, Published on 11th August, 2023  
**Jurisdiction:** Republic of India  
**Document Nature:** Official Statutory Text & Legal Mandates for Digital Processing Systems  

---

## 1. Statutory Definitions (Section 2)

* **Section 2(i) "Data Fiduciary":** Any person or entity who alone or in conjunction with other persons determines the purpose and means of processing of personal data. (In AgriTech: the platform provider, FPO digital platform, agri-enterprise, or banking partner).
* **Section 2(j) "Data Principal":** The individual to whom the personal data relates. (In AgriTech: the individual farmer, tenant farmer, grower, or agricultural worker). Where the individual is a child, it includes the parent or lawful guardian.
* **Section 2(k) "Data Processor":** Any person who processes personal data on behalf of a Data Fiduciary. (In AgriTech: cloud service providers, third-party drone spraying contractors, SMS gateway providers, or field data collection agencies).
* **Section 2(g) "Consent Manager":** A person registered with the Data Protection Board who acts as a single point of contact to enable a Data Principal to give, manage, review, and withdraw consent through an accessible, transparent, and interoperable platform.
* **Section 2(t) "Personal Data":** Any data about an individual who is identifiable by or in relation to such data.
* **Section 2(u) "Personal Data Breach":** Any unauthorised processing of personal data or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction of or loss of access to personal data, that compromises the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of personal data.

---

## 2. Notice and Grounds for Processing (Sections 4 & 5)

* **Section 4 (Lawful Processing):** A person may process the personal data of an individual only in accordance with the provisions of this Act and for a lawful purpose:
  1. For which the Data Principal has given her consent; or
  2. For certain legitimate uses specified under Section 7 (e.g., voluntary provision for a specified purpose, provision of government subsidies/benefits under PM-KISAN, compliance with court orders, or medical emergencies).
* **Section 5(1) (Notice Requirement):** Every request for consent under Section 6 must be accompanied or preceded by an itemised notice informing the Data Principal:
  1. The personal data to be collected and the specific purpose for which such data will be processed;
  2. The manner in which the Data Principal may exercise her rights under Section 11 (Access) and Section 12 (Correction and Erasure);
  3. The manner in which the Data Principal may file a complaint with the Data Protection Board.
* **Section 5(3) (Language Accessibility):** The Data Fiduciary must give the Data Principal the option to view the notice and consent request in **English or in any of the 22 languages specified in the Eighth Schedule to the Constitution of India** (essential for regional language farmer interfaces in Hindi, Marathi, Telugu, Kannada, Tamil, Punjabi, etc.).

---

## 3. Consent Architecture and Withdrawal (Section 6)

* **Section 6(1) (Consent Standard):** Consent given by the Data Principal must be:
  1. **Free:** Not coerced or bundled as a mandatory condition for an unrelated service.
  2. **Specific:** Tied to a clearly stated purpose (no blanket consent for undefined future analytics).
  3. **Informed:** Accompanied by the statutory notice under Section 5.
  4. **Unconditional:** Not conditioned on consenting to collection of data unnecessary for the core function.
  5. **Unambiguous:** Signified through a clear affirmative action.
* **Section 6(2) (Limitation to Specified Purpose):** Any part of consent which infringes upon the provisions of this Act shall be invalid to the extent of such infringement.
* **Section 6(4) (Right of Withdrawal):** The Data Principal has the right to withdraw consent at any time. **The ease of withdrawing consent must be comparable to the ease with which consent was given.**
* **Section 6(5) (Consequences of Withdrawal):** Once consent is withdrawn, the Data Fiduciary and its Data Processors must cease processing the personal data within a reasonable time, unless continued processing is required by another applicable law.
* **Section 6(9) (Burden of Proof):** The burden of proving that consent was given in accordance with the Act lies entirely upon the Data Fiduciary. Digital systems must maintain immutable, auditable electronic consent records (timestamp, version, notice text presented).

---

## 4. General Obligations of Data Fiduciaries (Section 8)

* **Section 8(4) (Data Accuracy):** The Data Fiduciary must make reasonable efforts to ensure that personal data processed is accurate, complete, and consistent if it is used to make a decision that affects the Data Principal (e.g., credit eligibility, crop insurance payouts, subsidy verification).
* **Section 8(5) (Reasonable Security Safeguards):** The Data Fiduciary must implement **reasonable security safeguards** to prevent personal data breaches in its possession or under its control, including across any Data Processors it engages.
* **Section 8(6) (Mandatory Breach Notification):** In the event of a personal data breach, the Data Fiduciary **must give intimation of such breach to the Data Protection Board of India and to each affected Data Principal** in the form and manner prescribed by Central Government rules.
* **Section 8(7) (Storage Limitation and Erasure):** A Data Fiduciary must erase personal data upon:
  1. The Data Principal withdrawing her consent; or
  2. As soon as it is reasonable to assume that the specified purpose for which data was collected is no longer being served, whichever is earlier, unless retention is mandated by law.
* **Section 8(10) (Grievance Redressal Mechanism):** The Data Fiduciary must establish an effective mechanism to redress grievances of Data Principals within prescribed statutory timelines.

---

## 5. Processing of Children's Data and Persons with Disabilities (Section 9)

* **Section 9(1) (Verifiable Parental Consent):** Before processing personal data of any child (an individual who has not completed 18 years of age) or person with disability, the Data Fiduciary must obtain **verifiable consent from the parent or lawful guardian**.
* **Section 9(2) (Detrimental Processing):** A Data Fiduciary must not undertake processing that is likely to cause any detrimental effect on the well-being of a child.
* **Section 9(3) (Prohibition on Tracking):** A Data Fiduciary **must not undertake tracking or behavioural monitoring of children or targeted advertising directed at children**.

---

## 6. Significant Data Fiduciary (SDF) Obligations (Section 10)

Entities designated as Significant Data Fiduciaries by the Central Government (based on volume of data, risk of harm, or national security) must:
1. Appoint a **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** based in India who reports to the Board of Directors;
2. Appoint an **Independent Data Auditor** to conduct regular data audits;
3. Undertake **Periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIA)** and security audits before deploying high-risk data systems or AI algorithms.

---

## 7. Rights of the Data Principal (Sections 11–14)

* **Section 11 (Right to Access Information):** The Data Principal has the right to obtain from the Data Fiduciary:
  1. A summary of personal data being processed and the processing activities undertaken;
  2. The identities of all other Data Fiduciaries and Data Processors with whom the personal data has been shared;
  3. Any other information as may be prescribed.
* **Section 12 (Right to Correction and Erasure):** The Data Principal has the right to correction of inaccurate or misleading data, completion of incomplete data, updating of obsolete data, and erasure of personal data that is no longer necessary.
* **Section 13 (Right of Grievance Redressal):** The Data Principal has the right to readily accessible grievance redressal with the Data Fiduciary before escalating to the Board.
* **Section 14 (Right to Nominate):** The Data Principal has the right to nominate another individual who shall exercise data rights in the event of death or incapacity.

---

## 8. Specific Architectural Mandates for AgriTech Engineering

1. **Assisted Farmer Interfaces & Consent Logging:** For rural farmers using shared devices or assisted kiosk operators (e.g., Common Service Centers / Village Level Entrepreneurs), consent cannot be assumed. Systems must implement OTP/biometric verification with clear visual/audio consent notices.
2. **Geo-Location and Plot Boundary Minimization:** Geo-fencing coordinates and drone spray traces must be stripped of direct personal identifiers when shared for aggregate research, logistics, or mandi price discovery.
3. **Data Retention Timelines:** Retain transactional and payment data for 5 to 7 years only where mandated by RBI/tax regulations; operational drone telemetry, raw imagery, and temporary advisory session data must have automated automated purge schedules under Section 8(7).
