# Soil health cards and agronomic advisory: orientation note

Document type: Paraphrased orientation note prepared for this project. NOT agronomic advice.
Jurisdiction: India
Official source to verify: Soil Health Card portal of the Department of Agriculture and Farmers Welfare, state agriculture universities, and Krishi Vigyan Kendras.
Status: NEEDS-VALIDATION. Any recommendation shown to farmers must be approved by qualified agronomists.

## What it is
The Soil Health Card scheme provides farmers with a report on the nutrient status of their soil with recommendations for nutrients and fertiliser use. Many apps present soil data, weather, and crop advisories, sometimes with AI-generated suggestions.

## Why advisory content is a safety topic
Wrong or poorly localised advice can reduce yield, waste money, damage soil, or cause chemical misuse. Advice depends on crop variety, soil type, growth stage, weather, local practice, and regulation. A generic model cannot guarantee correctness.

## Requirement prompts
- Who are the qualified experts that author and approve advice, and how is approval recorded and versioned?
- Is generated advice limited to approved content, with the source shown, and is there a clear message when the system does not know?
- How is advice localised by region, crop, season, and language?
- How are soil test data and lab results validated, and how are lab errors corrected?
- How is feedback from farmers and field staff captured to correct bad advice quickly?
- Which advice categories are blocked or require expert review (pesticide choice and dose, veterinary treatment, anything touching human or animal safety)?
- How is liability communicated, for example a disclaimer in simple language plus a contact for human help?
